Contraction strain

L₀ is the distance between the clips in the first tracked frame, when the sample is fully stretched under its hanging mass. L(t) is that distance at any later moment. Don’t confuse L₀ with the unstretched length you measured before mounting.

Contraction strain = [L₀ − L(t)] / L₀ × 100%

For example, shortening from 30 mm to 27 mm is 3 mm of contraction, or 10% strain. Positive values mean the sample has contracted. Within one cycle, the maximum strain minus the minimum strain is the peak-to-peak strain, the stroke the sample delivers each cycle.

Measuring current

The power supply display updates too slowly to follow fast switching, so above 1 Hz its reading can’t be trusted. For faster tests, run one 0.1 Hz cycle before the experiment and use that steady-state current. If the current falls by more than about 50 mA during a test, the connection is degrading.

Optional: oscilloscope check

The protocol doesn’t require an oscilloscope, but it’s a quick way to confirm the MOSFET is switching. For a standard oscilloscope with shared, earth-referenced probe grounds, connect it as shown. Check your instrument’s grounding first.

ProbeTip connectionGround connectionExpected signal
CH1MOSFET gateCommon return / sourceAbout 0–5 V square wave
CH2MOSFET drainSame common return / sourceNear 0 V when on; near the supply voltage when off
Both ground clips go to the common return.

Never attach a grounded probe clip to the drain or the supply positive; that can short the circuit through the oscilloscope. Differential or isolated measurements need a suitably configured instrument.

The gate trace confirms the command and the drain trace confirms the switching. Neither one measures the current through the sample.
